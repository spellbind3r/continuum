# Historical Coverage - All Cities

This document shows the historical periods covered for each city in the knowledge base.

## Coverage Summary

- **Total Cities**: 15
- **Total Historical Periods**: 56
- **Time Span**: 3100 BCE to 1920 CE
- **Categories per Period**: Politics, Culture, Religion, Overview

---

## Cities and Their Periods

### 1. **Rome** (5 periods) | -753 to 1650
- **Roman Kingdom** (-753 to -509): Seven kings, Etruscan influence, foundation myths
- **Roman Republic** (-509 to -27): Senate and consuls, Punic Wars, Cicero and Caesar
- **Roman Empire** (-27 to 476): Augustus to fall, Pax Romana, Christianity spreads
- **Medieval Rome** (476 to 1420): Papal States, center of Catholic Church
- **Renaissance Rome** (1420 to 1650): Michelangelo, Sistine Chapel, Counter-Reformation

### 2. **Athens** (4 periods) | -800 to 529
- **Archaic Athens** (-800 to -508): Solon's reforms, emergence of democracy
- **Classical Athens** (-508 to -323): Golden Age, Pericles, Socrates, Plato, Parthenon
- **Hellenistic Athens** (-323 to -146): Philosophical schools, cultural center
- **Roman Athens** (-146 to 529): University city, Hadrian's patronage

### 3. **Cairo** (4 periods) | -3100 to 1867
- **Ancient Memphis** (-3100 to -30): Pharaonic capital, Giza Pyramids, Temple of Ptah
- **Fatimid Cairo** (969 to 1171): City founded, Al-Azhar Mosque, Shi'a dynasty
- **Mamluk Cairo** (1250 to 1517): Islamic Golden Age, Khan el-Khalili, greatest Islamic city
- **Ottoman Cairo** (1517 to 1867): Ottoman province, Muhammad Ali reforms

### 4. **Baghdad** (4 periods) | 762 to 1917
- **Abbasid Golden Age** (762 to 1055): House of Wisdom, world's largest city, Islamic science
- **Seljuk Period** (1055 to 1258): Turkish military rule, caliphs as figureheads
- **Ilkhanate Period** (1258 to 1411): Mongol conquest, devastation and recovery
- **Ottoman Baghdad** (1534 to 1917): Ottoman-Persian conflicts, modernization

### 5. **Beijing** (4 periods) | -1045 to 1912
- **Ancient Ji** (-1045 to 221): Yan state capital, Zhou dynasty, northern frontier
- **Yuan Dynasty** (1271 to 1368): Kublai Khan's Dadu, Mongol rule, Marco Polo
- **Ming Dynasty** (1368 to 1644): Forbidden City built, Great Wall, cultural flowering
- **Qing Dynasty** (1644 to 1912): Manchu rule, zenith and decline, Boxer Rebellion

### 6. **London** (4 periods) | 43 to 1714
- **Roman Londinium** (43 to 410): Trading center, Boudica's revolt, Roman infrastructure
- **Medieval London** (886 to 1485): Norman Conquest, Tower of London, Chaucer
- **Tudor London** (1485 to 1603): Renaissance, Shakespeare, Reformation, Elizabeth I
- **Stuart London** (1603 to 1714): Great Fire, Plague, Civil War, Wren's rebuilding

### 7. **Paris** (4 periods) | -52 to 1789
- **Roman Lutetia** (-52 to 486): Gallic settlement becomes Roman town
- **Medieval Paris** (987 to 1453): University (Sorbonne), Notre-Dame, Gothic architecture
- **Renaissance Paris** (1453 to 1643): Francis I, religious wars, Louvre expansion
- **Ancien Régime** (1643 to 1789): Louis XIV, Enlightenment, Voltaire and Rousseau

### 8. **Delhi** (3 periods) | 1206 to 1947
- **Delhi Sultanate** (1206 to 1526): Five dynasties, Qutb Minar, Indo-Islamic culture
- **Mughal Delhi** (1526 to 1857): Red Fort, Jama Masjid, Shah Jahan's golden age
- **British Raj** (1857 to 1947): New Delhi built, independence movement, partition

### 9. **Istanbul** (4 periods) | 330 to 1922
- **Byzantine Constantinople** (330 to 1204): Hagia Sophia, Eastern Roman Empire, 1000 years
- **Latin Constantinople** (1204 to 1261): Fourth Crusade, brief Latin rule
- **Late Byzantine** (1261 to 1453): Restored empire, Palaiologian Renaissance
- **Ottoman Istanbul** (1453 to 1922): Mehmed II conquest, Ottoman capital, Topkapi Palace

### 10. **Venice** (3 periods) | 697 to 1630
- **Early Medieval** (697 to 1171): Lagoon city founded, maritime trade, Byzantine ties
- **Maritime Republic** (1171 to 1453): Mediterranean power, Marco Polo, Gothic palaces
- **Renaissance Venice** (1453 to 1630): Titian, Tintoretto, Venetian School, printing

### 11. **Kyiv** (3 periods) | 882 to 1917
- **Kievan Rus** (882 to 1240): First East Slavic state, Christianization, St. Sophia
- **Polish-Lithuanian** (1362 to 1667): Magdeburg rights, Cossack uprisings, Baroque
- **Imperial Russian** (1667 to 1917): Ukrainian national revival, Shevchenko, industrialization

### 12. **Jerusalem** (5 periods) | -516 to 1917
- **Second Temple** (-516 to 70): Jewish center, Herod's Temple, Jesus' ministry
- **Byzantine Jerusalem** (324 to 638): Christian holy city, Church of Holy Sepulchre
- **Early Islamic** (638 to 1099): Dome of the Rock, Al-Aqsa Mosque, third holiest city
- **Crusader Kingdom** (1099 to 1187): Latin Kingdom, Knights Templar, Saladin's reconquest
- **Mamluk-Ottoman** (1260 to 1917): Suleiman's walls, three faiths coexist

### 13. **Xi'an** (3 periods) | -221 to 907
- **Qin Dynasty** (-221 to -206): First unified China, Terracotta Army, Great Wall
- **Han Dynasty** (-206 to 220): Silk Road terminus, world's largest city, paper invented
- **Tang Dynasty** (618 to 907): Golden age, cosmopolitan city of 1 million, Li Bai poetry

### 14. **Mexico City** (3 periods) | 1325 to 1910
- **Aztec Tenochtitlan** (1325 to 1521): Island city, chinampas, Templo Mayor, human sacrifice
- **Spanish Colonial** (1521 to 1821): Viceroyalty of New Spain, Cathedral, mestizo culture
- **19th Century** (1821 to 1910): Independence, reforms, Porfirio Díaz modernization

### 15. **Cuzco** (3 periods) | 1200 to 1821
- **Early Inca** (1200 to 1438): Local kingdom, Quechua culture, terraced agriculture
- **Imperial Inca** (1438 to 1533): Tawantinsuyu capital, Sacsayhuamán, Machu Picchu
- **Spanish Colonial** (1533 to 1821): Pizarro's conquest, syncretism, Cuzco School art

## Quality Standards

Each period includes:

✅ **Overview**: 1-2 sentence description of the period
✅ **Politics**: Government structure, rulers, major events
✅ **Culture**: Architecture, literature, arts, language
✅ **Religion**: Dominant faiths, temples, religious life
✅ **Time Range**: Start and end years
✅ **Source Attribution**: Historical source reference
